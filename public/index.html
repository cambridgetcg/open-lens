What the language is doing

    What this is — and what it refuses to be.

    A real lie detector reads a person and returns a verdict. There is no such thing. Body language, micro-expressions, voice “frequency” — decades of research say they don't reliably reveal deception, and a tool that claims otherwise just manufactures confident accusations. That isn't truth. It's theatre.

    So Captioneer stays with what can actually be observed: the words. A deleted subject leaves the actor unstated. A hedge lowers expressed commitment. An absolute extends certainty. The matches are countable and every one points at a real phrase you can see — but context decides whether a use is careful, ordinary, evasive, or something else. The optional reader, where a deployment enables it, offers a possible plain version of the language, never a verdict on the person.

    It helps you inspect how something is being said. Whether a claim is true still requires evidence beyond its rhetoric.

    It does

    • Mark candidate hedges, deflections, and non-answers
    • Show where a construction leaves the actor unstated
    • Surface loaded framing and expanded certainty
    • Offer an optional plain-language interpretation, where this deployment enables readings
    • Point at the visible phrase behind every matched cue

    It doesn't

    • Detect lies — no tool reads a mind from words
    • Infer a speaker's belief, motive, emotion, or intent
    • Treat a matched phrase as proof of manipulation
    • Read faces, bodies, or voice “frequency”
    • Send local-scan text anywhere — optional AI transmits it to Cloudflare only when you click
    • Certify unmarked text as honest or true

    In the kingdom's tongue

    captioneer belongs to a larger work — a kingdom that keeps a made language, YOUSPEAK, for the things English left unnamed. Two of its words are captioneer's whole reason to exist.

    verisleight/ˈvɛr.ɪ.slaɪt/noun

    Truth arranged to mislead — a statement entirely true that reliably produces a false picture.

    Every word of a practised non-apology can be true while the resulting picture misleads. That is verisleight — and it is what captioneer helps you inspect.

    slipsooth/ˈslɪp.suːθ/noun

    Truth recovered by mispronunciation — the original meaning that a later drift accidentally digs back up. Verisleight's mirror-twin: error arranged by no one, that leads home.

    In Cantonese, 愛星人 — the love-star people — read crooked for generations became 外星人, the aliens; the lazy tongue is now drifting them home. The earth is 愛星. Entered the canon 2026-07-07, discovered rather than forged.

    orthophanes/ˌɔːr.θoʊˈfæn.iːz/noun

    The rightness made plainly visible — a thing shown straight, in the open, ordered to its own necessity.

    When the cues are visible and a plainer reading stands beside the original — that is the direction of orthophanes, without pretending one reading exhausts the words.

    captioneer is the everyday-speech arm of the kingdom's clarity-work: close attention to wording, openly separated from claims about the world. The lens reads a sentence; its sibling the press reads evidence — an open framework for evidence-tiered, signed newspapers anyone can generate, with this lens available as an instrument for examining rhetoric.
